CARELINK SYSTEM GUIDE
====================

This document explains how the CareLink system works and how to use it as a user. It is written so a panelist, evaluator, or stakeholder can understand the full flow of the platform quickly.


1. WHAT CARELINK IS
-------------------

CareLink is a smart health referral and queue management system designed for barangay health centers, city health offices, and administrators.

It helps:
- register patients
- create and review medical referrals
- prioritize urgent cases
- track queue status in real time
- notify patients through SMS and email
- generate reports and analytics
- monitor patient status using public tracking links

The platform is built for local health operations, especially in referral-based patient care.


2. MAIN USERS / ROLES
---------------------

The system is role-based. Different user types can access different features.

A. Barangay Staff
- Registers patients
- Submits referral requests
- Reviews and manages local records
- Can access patient and referral modules

B. City Staff / Review Staff
- Reviews incoming referrals from barangay centers
- Manages queue prioritization
- Sees analytics and reports
- Can approve, complete, or mark referrals as missed/rejected

C. Super Admin
- Full system access
- Manages users and settings
- Oversees audit logs, SMS/email activity
- Configures health centers and system rules

D. Public / Patient
- Uses tracking link to check referral progress
- Does not need staff login
- Can view queue and tracking status


2.1 PURPOSE OF RBAC (ROLE-BASED ACCESS CONTROL)
--------------------------------------------------

RBAC is used to control what each user can do in the system based on their assigned role.

The purpose of RBAC is to:
- protect sensitive health and referral information
- avoid unauthorized access to data and operations
- segment responsibilities between barangay staff, city staff, and administrators
- keep patient data secure and compliant with operational privacy expectations
- reduce mistakes by limiting functions to the appropriate role

In simple terms, RBAC ensures that a user can only access the modules and actions required for their responsibilities.

Example:
- Barangay staff can register patients and create referrals.
- City staff can review and process queue actions.
- Super Admin can manage the entire system.
- Public users can only view tracking information, not internal records.


2.2 DETAILED ROLE ACCESS
------------------------

A. ADMIN ACCESS (Super Admin)
- Full access to all system modules and pages.
- Can view Dashboard, Patients, Referrals, Queue, Analytics, Tracking, Evaluation, Profile, and Admin.
- Can manage system users and assign roles.
- Can configure system settings and health center data.
- Can access audit logs, SMS logs, email logs, and activity history.
- Can monitor all referrals, queue entries, and patient data across the system.
- Can change system-level rules and permissions when necessary.

This role is responsible for overall system governance and security.

B. CITY STAFF ACCESS
- Can review and oversee referrals submitted by barangay units.
- Can access queue and operational monitoring screens.
- Can view analytics and performance reports.
- Can approve, update, complete, or reassign referral status when needed.
- Can mark referrals as missed, rejected, expired, or invalid queue entries.
- Can manage active queue operations and prioritize urgent cases.
- Cannot modify system settings or full user administration.

This role focuses on referral processing and service delivery.

C. BARANGAY STAFF ACCESS
- Can register patient records into the system.
- Can create and submit referral requests.
- Can manage patient and referral information related to their assigned health center.
- Can track local records and referral progress for their patients.
- Can view patient details and referral records needed for barangay operations.
- Cannot access full admin controls or system-wide configuration tools.

This role focuses on front-line patient intake and referral submission.

D. PUBLIC / PATIENT ACCESS
- Can access only public tracking features.
- Can check referral status by entering a tracking code or using a provided tracking link.
- Can view queue status or patient progress information related to their referral.
- Cannot access internal staff dashboards, patient database, admin screens, or system settings.

This is the public-facing part of the system for transparency and patient communication.


2.3 RBAC ACCESS SUMMARY
------------------------

| Role | Main Access | Restricted Access |
|------|-------------|------------------|
| Barangay Staff | Patients, Referrals, local records | Full admin controls, system-wide analytics and settings |
| City Staff | Queue, monitoring, analytics, referral review | User management and system settings |
| Super Admin | Full system, users, settings, logs, audits | None |
| Public / Patient | Tracking link and status lookup | Internal records, staff dashboards, admin features |

This access model keeps the system organized, secure, and easier to manage.

4. OVERVIEW OF THE SYSTEM INTERFACE
-----------------------------------

After logging in, the system shows a sidebar with major modules:

- Dashboard
- Patients
- Referrals
- Queue
- Analytics
- Tracking
- Evaluation
- Profile

The exact tabs shown depend on the user's role.


5. DASHBOARD
-------------

The Dashboard is the home screen for management and monitoring.

It presents:
- overall referral summary
- queue counts
- patient numbers
- referral status overview
- performance indicators
- AI or analytics summaries

This is where users get a quick picture of current operations.


6. PATIENT REGISTRATION MODULE
------------------------------

This module is used to add patient information into the system.

What the staff enters may include:
- first name, middle name, last name
- birth date
- sex
- contact number and email
- address and barangay details
- medical classifications
- emergency contact information
- medical notes

Important points:
- The system validates required information.
- Barangay staff are typically assigned to the health center they belong to.
- The patient record is linked to the correct health center and local barangay.

This creates a patient profile that can later be selected when submitting a referral.


7. REFERRAL MODULE
------------------

The Referrals tab is the heart of the system.

How it works:
1. The user selects an existing patient.
2. A referral record is created for a medical or follow-up case.
3. The system records the reason for referral, urgency, and severity.
4. It determines the receiving health center and priority level.
5. The referral is saved and queued for processing.

Referral fields may include:
- patient ID
- reason for referral
- referral type
- clinical urgency
- severity level
- receiving health center
- status
- priority classification

The system distinguishes between:
- Submitted
- Under review
- Queued
- Completed
- Missed
- Rejected
- Expired
- Archived

The referral status helps identify whether the case is still pending, active, or closed.


8. REFERRAL PRIORITY AND URGENCY
--------------------------------

The platform uses different levels of urgency and filtering.

Urgency examples:
- Routine
- Urgent
- Emergency

Priority examples:
- Priority 1 Emergency
- Priority 2 Vulnerable
- Priority 3 Standard

This allows the system to decide which patients should be served first based on medical need and vulnerability.


9. QUEUE MANAGEMENT MODULE
---------------------------

The Queue page is where real-time processing happens.

It shows all active or filtered referrals in line for service.

Features include:
- active queue view
- range filters (active, today, custom dates, etc.)
- status filters (waiting, called, served, missed, cancelled, expired)
- referral status filters
- search by patient name or code
- priority sorting

Staff can:
- review the queue
- call a patient
- send SMS/email notification to the patient
- mark queue entries as served, missed, or invalid
- update referral outcomes

Queue management is designed to ensure that the most urgent or vulnerable patient is served first.

When a patient is called, the system may send a notification message to the patient to inform them that their appointment or consultation is ready.


10. HOW THE SYSTEM DECIDES WHO IS CALLED FIRST
-----------------------------------------------

The queue is not random. It is organized using patient priority and referral status.

The system considers:
- clinical urgency
- severity level
- vulnerability classification
- referral queue status
- date/time range
- whether the patient is still active in queue

This helps city staff focus on the right patients at the right time and reduces delays in referral care.


11. SMS AND EMAIL NOTIFICATIONS
--------------------------------

The system sends automated messages for important updates.

Examples:
- referral submitted or approved
- patient called for consultation
- appointment reminder sent
- missed referral alert
- status update for tracking

The system uses SMS and email to notify both staff and patients. This reduces communication delays and improves patient follow-up.


12. PUBLIC TRACKING AND PATIENT STATUS CHECK
---------------------------------------------

One of the most important parts of the system is public tracking.

Each referral generates a tracking code, and a patient can use a tracking link to check progress.

Example:
https://carelink-bay.vercel.app/track/CL-YYYYMMDD-ABC123

The patient can enter the code or click the link in the SMS to see:
- referral status
- queue progress
- appointment information
- whether the patient is waiting, called, served, or expired

This feature allows patients and family members to know where the referral stands without needing staff access.

The Admin page is intended for super administrators.

This module may include:

17. FULL USAGE FLOW (EXAMPLE)
-----------------------------

Here is a typical real-world workflow:

Step 1: Staff logs in
- Barangay staff signs in using their account.

Step 2: Patient registration
- Staff registers a patient with complete profile details.

Step 3: New referral creation
- The patient is selected and a referral request is created.

Step 4: Referral review
- City staff reviews the referral and checks urgency, severity, and reason.

Step 5: Queue assignment
- The referral is queued based on medical priority and vulnerability.

Step 6: Patient notification
- The patient receives SMS/email notification and a tracking code.

Step 7: Queue operational use
- The queue screen shows patient position and status.

Step 8: Call and service
- Staff call the patient when they are next in line.

Step 9: Completion or update
- The referral is marked completed, missed, rejected, or archived.

Step 10: Tracking and reporting
- The patient sees the status via tracking link.
- Admins view analytics and download reports.


18. BEST PRACTICES FOR USERS
---------------------------

- Always register the patient completely before creating a referral.
- Review the referral reason and severity carefully.
- Check queue priority before calling patients.
- Use public tracking links to answer patient inquiries.
- Make sure notifications are sent when queue status changes.
- Use analytics to monitor performance and identify delays.


19. COMMON TERMS IN THE SYSTEM
-----------------------------

Patient Record
- A record containing patient identification and profile information.

Referral
- A request to send a patient to another health facility or service.

Queue
- The list of active referrals waiting for service.

Tracking Code
- A unique code used to check referral status.

Priority Level
- The urgency or vulnerability category used to decide who gets served first.

Status
- Current condition of the referral or queue entry.


20. SUMMARY
-----------

CareLink is a full digital health referral and queue management platform that connects patient registration, referral processing, queue handling, tracking, notifications, analytics, and admin control in one system.

The core idea is simple:
- register the patient
- submit a referral
- prioritize the case
- notify the patient
- track progress
- manage outcomes
- review data and reports

This workflow allows health services to respond faster, organize better, and provide a more transparent referral experience for both staff and patients.
